## What S2 and the M3.1 S3 consumers must still do

The helpers are prerequisites, not ports. Each consumer keeps the obligations
the M0 reviews name at its own site:

- Replace the Java baseline's reflective walkers with explicit traversal that
  follows `IrModel.walked`, compare it with the reflective result, then port
  it with an exhaustive switch over `IrModel.Record` (S2's exit).
- Write each consumer's own fixture from WORKLIST_CONTRACTS, CALLBACK_CONTRACTS
  and SORTING_CONTRACTS (nested scopes, restore order, first-match ties,
  callback timing) and prove each hash-container traversal's order or
  order-independence before its first observable choice (M0 S1 rule).
- Keep the D261/D262 forms: creation-array owners, no service getters,
  caller-frame builders, invocation-lived shared graphs.
- Natural `Path` order stays blocked at its B2/B3 consumers (M4/M5); no M3.1
  consumer needs it.

## Retained limits

These are conservative rejections, not safety gaps:

- Sorted lists, stacks, queues and fixed-arity lists expose their items, which
  then stay invocation-lived; the containers themselves retire.
- Captured callback state stays invocation-lived; holders free before the
  callbacks they retain.
- The M1 copy-lent and D253 escape-summary limits recorded at G1 still apply.
- Primitive `Arrays.sort` remains an insertion sort; only small Integer
  natural-order inputs use it.

## Handoff run

One run on a fresh `git archive` of the handoff commit: the strict
`scripts/build.sh`, `javac --release 21 -Xlint:all -Werror` over every
compiler test source, and 31 focused tests: the eleven M3.1 tests, the eight
ArrayList consumers of D263 and the twelve M1/M2 consumers of `Lists.iron`
(the three value-helper tests and the nine M2 pilot tests). The classification
and the diff and license audits also ran. Unsafe programs were compile-only;
no full suite or hosted build ran.
